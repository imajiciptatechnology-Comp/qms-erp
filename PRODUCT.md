# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Cross-department staff (HR, production, engineering, QA managers, ISO coordinators) — each using the module relevant to their role. Department heads submit and review records; QA owns oversight. Users range from technically confident QA officers to non-technical production staff who may touch the system infrequently. Every screen must be legible and actionable without training.

## Product Purpose

A Quality Management System ERP for ISO 9001:2015 compliance. Centralizes training records, supplier evaluations, customer complaints, quality objectives, risk registers, non-conformances, internal audits, and document control. Success means every department can demonstrate ISO compliance without hunting through spreadsheets or email threads.

## Brand Personality

Professional, trustworthy, precise. The system handles compliance data that gets presented to external auditors — it must feel authoritative and accurate. Not playful, not flashy. Calm under pressure: users often open this during audits or incidents.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboard starter template (blue sidebar, icon-card grid, gradient hero metrics)
- Consumer/mobile-app aesthetic (illustrations, bright playful colors, oversized CTAs)

## Design Principles

1. **Data before decoration** — every visual choice must serve the data, not decorate the page. If a design element doesn't help users find or act on information, remove it.
2. **Authoritative at a glance** — the UI should read as official. Clean hierarchy, consistent typographic scale, no ambiguous affordances.
3. **Departmental clarity** — each module is owned by a different team. Module identity (accent color, icon, name) must be distinct and instantly scannable across the app.
4. **Audit-ready output** — tables, detail pages, and print views will be reviewed by ISO auditors. Density and completeness matter; whitespace is earned, not defaulted.
5. **Approachable for infrequent users** — production staff who open this once a month should not feel lost. Labels, empty states, and confirmations must be written for the non-expert.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Sufficient color contrast on all text and interactive elements. Keyboard navigation for all forms and modals. Status indicators must not rely on color alone (pair with text or icon).
